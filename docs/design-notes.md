- **Meenam:** twin fish as a small motif (invitation divider, welcome kolam, stage edge). The fish is also the Pandya emblem. Turmeric yellow and gold suit Guru.
- **Simmam:** golden sun-ray halo behind the vel, in saffron and gold. Lion motifs as small accents.
- **Revathi (Mercury):** green accents such as banana leaves, mango leaves and emerald peacock tones.
- **Pooram (Venus):** pink and white flowers (rose, jasmine, lotus), traditionally linked with love and marriage.

## Tamil Hindu wedding elements for visual inspiration

“Vayaimaram” is understood here as **vaazhai maram (வாழைமரம், banana tree)**. Banana stems or whole plants are commonly used to frame entrances and wedding spaces as auspicious, abundant greenery. Confirm the exact form and placement with the families or decorators.

These are a menu of familiar Tamil Hindu wedding references, not a checklist. Practices and meanings differ across Tamil regions, religious communities, and family traditions. Use only the elements that reflect the couple's own ceremony.

### Setting and decoration

- **Vaazhai maram (வாழைமரம்):** banana plants or stems at the entrance, pandal, or manavarai.
- **Pandal and manavarai / manamedai (பந்தல், மணவரை / மணமேடை):** the decorated wedding canopy and ceremony platform; suitable as the main composition for an invitation or hero illustration.
- **Maavilai thoranam (மாவிலைத் தோரணம்):** mango-leaf garlands across the entrance or canopy.
- **Kolam (கோலம்):** rice-flour or coloured threshold/floor design; can inspire a border or invitation divider. Keep sacred imagery out of places people walk on.
- **Kuthuvilakku (குத்துவிளக்கு):** traditional standing oil lamp, often decorated with flowers and lit for the ceremony.
- **Kalasam (கலசம்):** decorated ritual pot, often with leaves and coconut; use as a small ceremonial detail if appropriate to the family's practice.

### Flowers, textiles, and ceremonial details

- **Flowers:** jasmine/malli (மல்லி), kanakambaram (கனகாம்பரம்), roses, marigolds, and local seasonal flowers; use a restrained garland or floral-border treatment.
- **Wedding textiles:** silk saree, veshti, angavastram, and woven borders can inform fabric textures and gold detailing.
- **Seer varisai (சீர் வரிசை):** family gifts and offerings arranged on trays; fruit, coconuts, sweets, flowers, and vessels can inspire a still-life illustration. The contents vary by family.
- **Paruppu thengai (பருப்பு தேங்காய்):** paired cone-shaped sweet displays used in some Tamil weddings; a recognisable optional decorative detail.
- **Muhurtha pathirikai (முகூர்த்தப் பத்திரிகை):** wedding invitation tradition; useful reference for a formal border, auspicious opening, and family-forward wording.

### Ceremony moments (family custom dependent)

- **Maalai maatral (மாலை மாற்றல்):** exchanging garlands.
- **Oonjal (ஊஞ்சல்):** the couple seated on a decorated swing in communities/families that observe it.
- **Nalangu (நலங்கு):** playful blessing and anointing rituals, often held before or after the main ceremony depending on custom.
- **Muhurtham and thali / mangalyam tying (முகூர்த்தம், தாலி):** the auspicious time and central marriage moment in many Tamil Hindu ceremonies.
- **Homam, pori iduthal, ammi midithal, and sapthapathi:** fire offerings, puffed rice, stepping on a grinding stone, and seven steps; the sequence and inclusion depend on the family's tradition.
- **Nadaswaram and thavil (நாதஸ்வரம், தவில்):** ceremonial music that can inspire a sound or instrument motif in printed materials.

### Suggested composition for this couple

- Entrance or page border: a simple kolam geometry, mango-leaf thoranam, and two banana stems.
- Ceremony focal point: a manavarai with a restrained gold vel and sun-ray halo, flanked by peacock-feather greens.
- Supporting details: kuthuvilakku, jasmine garland, silk weave, and one small kalasam or paruppu-thengai illustration.
- Keep fish, water ripples, sun rays, and lion accents subtle so the invitation still reads first as a wedding invitation.
- Welcome sign: “வேலும் மயிலும் துணை”.

## Respect notes

The vel and peacock are sacred. Keep them on the stage or backdrop, away from floors, food counters and napkins, and don't place them where people will step.

Confirm ritual terminology, sequence, and any religious symbols with both families and the officiant before using them as literal ceremony details. This document is a visual planning guide, not a ritual prescription.
